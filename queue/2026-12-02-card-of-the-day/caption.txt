One blue mana, and every noncreature spell your opponents cast either draws you a card or makes them find four.

Mystic Remora is about the best turn-one play blue has and one of the worst turn-six ones. Nothing actually costs more, by the way: the opponent just chooses whether to pay four and stop the draw. Early, nobody can spare it, so you draw. Late, everyone shrugs and pays, and you're left holding a card that charges you rent.

Cumulative upkeep means it costs one, then two, then three, then four. Three or four cards off it is a good run. Five is a great one. Letting it fall off is the plan, not a failure — people treat it like a permanent they have to protect and that's how it ends up costing more than it draws.

It's the priciest card in this fortnight's posts, and it still undercuts most blue draw engines doing half as much work.
